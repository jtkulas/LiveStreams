Admonitions (also called callouts, alerts, or notices) are highlighted blocks for notes, tips, warnings, etc. Your Quarto extension uses the simple AsciiDoc-style paragraph syntax (NOTE:, WARNING:, etc.). There is no single universal standard, but many documentation systems and markup languages define overlapping sets of types, often with a severity hierarchy. The core five that appear most frequently are Note, Tip, Important, Caution, and Warning.

Major systems and their types

AsciiDoc (the direct model for your extension)
Types: NOTE, TIP, IMPORTANT, CAUTION, WARNING.
Syntax: WARNING: text for a single paragraph, or [WARNING] + block delimiters for complex content.
Distinction: Use CAUTION to advise acting carefully; use WARNING for actual danger, harm, or consequences.

Quarto Callouts
Types: note, tip, important, caution, warning (nearly identical to AsciiDoc).
Syntax: ::: {.callout-note} ... ::: (more verbose than the paragraph style your extension adds).

GitHub Alerts (and similar in GitLab)
Types: NOTE, TIP, IMPORTANT, WARNING, CAUTION.
Syntax: blockquote starting with > [!NOTE] (or the other types). These are a platform extension, not part of CommonMark or GFM.

reStructuredText / Sphinx
Types: attention, caution, danger, error, hint, important, note, tip, warning, seealso.
Syntax: .. note:: (or other type) as a directive. These are classic and widely used in Python and technical docs.

MkDocs Material
Types (with aliases): note, abstract/summary/tldr, info, tip/hint/important, success, question, warning/caution/attention, failure/error, danger, bug, example, quote.
Syntax: !!! note (or ??? for collapsible) with indented content. Highly customizable with icons and colors.

Obsidian Callouts
Types (with aliases): note, abstract/summary/tldr, info, todo, tip/hint/important, success/check/done, question/help/faq, warning/caution/attention, failure/fail/missing, danger/error, bug, example, quote/cite.
Syntax: > [!note] (or with +/- for open/collapsed by default, plus optional title). Very flexible for personal knowledge bases.

Docusaurus
Types: note, tip, info, warning, danger (plus some aliases such as caution).
Syntax: :::note … :::.

MyST Markdown (used in Jupyter Book and similar)
Types: note, attention, important, caution, hint, warning, seealso, tip, danger, error.
Similar in spirit to Sphinx directives, with good compatibility notes for other systems.

DITA
Uses a <note> element with a type attribute. Common values include note, tip, important, caution, danger, warning, notice, attention, remember, restriction, and others. Some types (especially danger/warning/notice) are aligned with ANSI Z535 and ISO 3864 safety standards. There is also a more structured <hazardstatement> for formal safety messaging.

Safety and style-guide standards

ANSI Z535.6 (product safety information in manuals and collateral materials) defines signal words with a clear severity hierarchy:
- DANGER — will result in death or serious injury
- WARNING — could result in death or serious injury
- CAUTION — may result in minor or moderate injury
- NOTICE — no personal injury (property damage or important non-hazard information)

These influence many technical-writing systems (including parts of DITA and Metanorma). Related standards such as ISO 3864 cover similar concepts.

Google Developer Documentation Style Guide recommends: Note, Caution, Warning, and Success. It emphasizes using them sparingly.

Metanorma extends AsciiDoc with additional types such as DANGER and SAFETY PRECAUTION.

Practical notes for a session

  • The five-type set (Note / Tip / Important / Caution / Warning) is the most portable and widely recognized.
  • Syntax is highly fragmented in Markdown (no CommonMark standard yet; there have been long-running discussions about directives or blockquote-based alerts). AsciiDoc, RST, and DITA are more standardized.
  • Severity hierarchies and visual cues (color, icon) vary by tool, but the underlying intent (supplemental info → tips → important constraints → cautions → warnings) is consistent.
  • Useful references to cite: AsciiDoc docs (for the syntax your extension emulates), Sphinx/RST, MkDocs Material, Obsidian, DITA language reference, and ANSI Z535.6 for the safety-oriented side.

These cover the main specifications and conventions you can reference alongside Quarto callouts and GitHub alerts.
